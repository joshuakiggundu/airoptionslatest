What Size AC Do You Need? A Simple HP/BTU Guide for Kampala Homes

One of the most common questions we get asked before an installation is simple: "What size AC do I actually need?" Get it wrong, and you either pay more than necessary for a unit that's too powerful, or end up with one that runs constantly and still doesn't cool the room properly. Here's how sizing actually works, in plain terms.

Why Size Matters More Than People Think

AC size is measured in horsepower (HP) or BTU (British Thermal Units) — this refers to cooling capacity, not physical size. A unit that's too small for a room will run non-stop trying to keep up, wearing out faster and never quite cooling the space. A unit that's too large cools the room too quickly without properly removing humidity, cycles on and off frequently, and costs more upfront and to run than necessary. The right size is the one matched to your actual room, not the cheapest or the most powerful option on the shelf.

A Simple HP Guide by Room Size

1.0 HP — suited to smaller rooms, roughly up to 12 square metres. Ideal for a single bedroom or small home office.
1.5 HP — suited to medium rooms, roughly up to 18 square metres. A common choice for standard bedrooms and small living rooms.
2.0 HP — suited to larger rooms, roughly up to 25 square metres. Common for living rooms, larger offices, and small shops.
2.5 HP and above — suited to open-plan spaces, larger offices, and commercial areas beyond 25 square metres.

These figures are a useful starting point, but they're not the full picture — a proper assessment accounts for more than floor area alone.

What Else Affects the Right Size for You

Ceiling height
A room with a higher-than-average ceiling has more air volume to cool, even if the floor area is the same as a standard room.

Sun exposure
A room with large windows facing direct sun, or an upper floor under a hot roof, needs more cooling capacity than a similarly sized, shaded room.

Number of people and heat-generating equipment
Offices with several computers, or rooms that regularly hold more people than average, generate additional heat that a standard sizing guide won't account for.

How the room is used
A bedroom used mainly at night has different cooling needs than a shop or office in constant daytime use.

The Most Common Sizing Mistake

The most common mistake we see is choosing a unit based on price or on what worked in someone else's space, rather than what the room actually requires. Two rooms of the same floor area can need different unit sizes depending on ceiling height, sun exposure, and use — which is exactly why guides like this one are a starting point, not a substitute for an actual assessment.
