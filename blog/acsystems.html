Multi-Split vs. Ductless Mini-Split vs. Central AC

Choosing the right air conditioning system starts with understanding how each type actually works — and which one fits your space, budget, and needs. Here's a clear comparison of the three main options to help you decide.

Ductless Mini-Split Systems

How it works: One outdoor unit connects to indoor units via slim refrigerant piping — no ductwork required.

Best for: Single rooms or additions, homes without existing ductwork, retrofits where installing ducts would be disruptive, and budget-conscious installations.

Key advantage: Fast, minimally invasive installation with no construction required.

Multi-Split Systems

How it works: Similar to ductless mini-split, but designed to support more indoor units (up to 8 or more) from a single outdoor unit, giving each room independent temperature control.

Best for: Mid-sized to large homes needing multiple zones, offices with several rooms requiring individual climate control, and boutique hospitality.

Key advantage: Whole-property, room-by-room comfort from a single compact outdoor unit.

Central Air Conditioning

How it works: One system distributes conditioned air throughout an entire building via ductwork, providing uniform cooling from a single control point.

Best for: Large homes, offices, and institutions needing consistent, whole-space cooling.

Key advantage: No visible indoor units cluttering rooms, and centralized control for the entire building.

How to Decide

Ask yourself: How many rooms need cooling? Do you have existing ductwork, or want to avoid installing it? Do you want individual room control, or uniform whole-building comfort? What's your installation budget and timeline?

One room points toward ductless mini-split. Several rooms point toward multi-split. The whole building points toward central AC.
