Signs You Need AC Repair vs. Replacement

  • Home
  • Blog
  • AC Repair vs. Replacement

Every AC system eventually shows signs of wear — but not every problem means you need a brand-new unit. Knowing the difference between a fixable issue and a system nearing the end of its life can save you money and prevent an unexpected breakdown during Kampala's hottest days.

Here's how to tell which situation you're in.

Signs You Likely Need a Repair

1. Reduced Cooling Performance
If your AC is running but not cooling as effectively as before, this is often a fixable issue — low refrigerant, a dirty filter, or a failing component are common, repairable causes.

2. Unusual Noises
Rattling, buzzing, or grinding sounds usually point to a specific mechanical issue rather than a system-wide failure. Most noise issues are diagnosable and repairable.

3. Water Leaks
A leaking indoor unit is almost always caused by a blocked drain line or a cracked drain pan — both straightforward repairs, not signs your system needs replacing.

4. Higher-Than-Usual Electricity Bills
A sudden spike in electricity costs, without a change in usage, often means your system is working harder than it should due to a fixable inefficiency.

5. The System Is Under 10 Years Old
Most well-maintained AC systems have a useful life of 10-15 years. If your unit falls within this range and has been maintained regularly, repair is almost always the more cost-effective choice.

Signs You Should Consider Replacement

1. Frequent, Repeated Breakdowns
If you're calling for repairs multiple times a year, the cumulative repair cost may already exceed what a new, more reliable system would cost.

2. The System Is Older Than 10-15 Years
Older systems become less efficient over time, and replacement parts can become harder to source.

3. Repair Costs Approach Replacement Costs
A common rule of thumb: if a single repair costs more than 30-50% of a new system's price, replacement is usually the smarter investment.

4. Rising Energy Bills Despite Repairs
If your electricity costs keep climbing even after repairs, your system may simply be too inefficient to run economically compared to a newer model.

5. Outdated Refrigerant Type
Older systems using outdated or increasingly restricted refrigerant types can face expensive future maintenance as those refrigerants become harder to source.

When in Doubt, Get an Assessment

The clearest way to know which category your system falls into is a professional inspection. A technician can evaluate your unit's condition, age, and repair history, and give you an honest recommendation — rather than guessing based on symptoms alone.
